# Evolutionary Evidence Synthesis

Cross-disciplinary research at the intersection of **Earth history, biological evolution, developmental biology, paleontology, genomics, and paleoclimate**.

This research pilot uses the breadth of modern language models to connect findings across disciplinary boundaries, identify tensions between established results, and formulate focused scientific questions that can be addressed with published evidence, mathematical reasoning, and public data.

## Research objective

**Find one precise evolutionary question whose answer becomes clearer when evidence from several fields is considered together.**

Promising outcomes include:
- A newly articulated tension between independently supported findings.
- A testable explanation reconciling geological, fossil, phylogenetic, or genomic evidence.
- A mathematical constraint on an established evolutionary scenario.
- A reproducible reanalysis of public data that distinguishes competing hypotheses.

The pilot begins with literature synthesis and converges on **one tractable research target**.

## Literature coverage

Review recent research across six connected areas, with **at least 100 papers screened in each area**:

| Area | Illustrative questions |
| --- | --- |
| Geology and stratigraphy | Geological timescales, tectonic barriers, land connections, preservation contexts |
| Evolutionary biology | Diversification, extinction, dispersal, adaptation, phylogenetic inference |
| Developmental biology | Developmental constraints, evo-devo, morphological innovation |
| Population genetics and genomics | Ancient DNA, gene flow, demographic history, adaptation, lineage discordance |
| Paleontology and the fossil record | Occurrence ranges, taxonomy, dating, taphonomy, sampling |
| Environment and paleoclimate | Climate reconstruction, biome shifts, ecological responses, sea level |

Start with recent authoritative reviews, then trace primary studies, underlying evidence, citations, and competing interpretations. Screen papers broadly and read the most relevant original studies in depth.

The six areas define a literature search space. Individual scientific questions may involve any subset of these areas.

## Working method

1. **Map the evidence.** Identify established results, open questions, major datasets, and competing interpretations.
2. **Look for tensions.** Compare claims that concern the same lineage, event, geography, or time interval; examine disagreements in dates, ranges, mechanisms, and assumptions.
3. **Resolve the measurement context.** Check geological age conventions, dating uncertainty, taxonomic revisions, sampling and preservation bias, spatial reconstruction, and model assumptions.
4. **Formulate candidate questions.** Express each candidate as a precise claim or constraint with a feasible test.
5. **Select one target.** Use primary literature, mathematical analysis, and a small reproducible calculation when useful.
6. **Write a research report.** Present the key evidence, the proposed connection, its interpretation, and a concrete route to testing it.

The emphasis is on **scientific connections and direct research**, with a lightweight workflow.

## Deliverable

A single research report containing a focused question, cited evidence from the relevant fields, a comparison of competing explanations, and a proposed verification plan. See [RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md) for the work program and [REPORT_TEMPLATE.md](REPORT_TEMPLATE.md) for the report structure.
