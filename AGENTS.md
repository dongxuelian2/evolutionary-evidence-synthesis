# Repository working instructions

## Mission

Conduct a focused cross-disciplinary literature investigation into evolutionary history. Use broad knowledge synthesis to discover a precise, testable research question from geological, fossil, developmental, genomic, phylogenetic, and environmental evidence.

The research workflow centers on reading, reasoning, source verification, and small targeted analyses. Maintain a lean repository organized around the scientific question and its evidence.

## Writing and language

- Write all repository-authored prose in **English**: README, research notes, reports, comments, and pull-request descriptions.
- Use direct, affirmative, evidence-centered statements. Lead with the research finding, method, assumption, result, or next concrete question.
- Remove defensive framing, repeated disclaimers, and self-exculpatory contrasts. Examples of excluded rhetorical patterns include: *not a*, *is not*, *does not constitute*, *does not imply*, *rather than*, *not yet*, *we do not claim*, *merely*, *only preliminary*, *beyond the scope*, and their paraphrases.
- Preserve essential logical negation in definitions, mathematical statements, tests, uncertainty descriptions, and source quotations. Accuracy takes priority over prose style.
- State evidentiary status precisely: **published observation**, **model-dependent inference**, **hypothesis**, **verified calculation**, or **open question**.

## Literature and scientific integrity

- Screen at least **100 papers in each of the six domains** specified in `RESEARCH.md`.
- Begin with major reviews. Follow cited and citing papers into specific controversies and primary evidence.
- Record source identity and provenance for each substantive claim. Verify DOI, author, date, quoted result, and the paper's actual scope.
- Distinguish observation from interpretation, stratigraphic age from molecular divergence time, specimen occurrence from range extent, and spatial association from causality.
- Identify shared assumptions and data dependencies before treating two published findings as independent support.
- Investigate apparent contradictions through dating ranges, taxonomy, phylogenetic model choice, stratigraphic completeness, sampling effort, and geological reconstruction.
- Check whether a candidate hypothesis or result has already been published.

## Research execution

- Prioritize a **single tractable question** supported by a cross-field evidence chain.
- Use equations, direct calculations, and existing open datasets where they sharpen the argument.
- Keep calculations reproducible and compatible with Linux and other common environments.
- Favor a complete, source-checked research report as the primary deliverable. Add code, dependencies, and infrastructure in proportion to the needs of the selected problem.
- Make substantive changes in focused commits or pull requests and maintain readable research records.
