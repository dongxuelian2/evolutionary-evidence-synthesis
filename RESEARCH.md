# Research program

## 1. Establish broad coverage efficiently

For each of the six domains listed in the README:

- Screen **at least 100 research papers**, prioritizing recent work and influential foundational results.
- Start with high-quality narrative reviews, systematic reviews, and field-wide syntheses.
- Build a thematic map of active debates, key methods, established timelines, major datasets, and unsolved questions.
- Expand toward primary studies through backward citation tracking, forward citations, and targeted searches.
- Read selected primary papers closely, especially methods, dating assumptions, datasets, supplemental material, and competing explanations.

A screened paper has an identifiable source, a verified title/identifier, and an extracted claim relevant to the research landscape. A deeply reviewed paper additionally has its central methods, evidence, and uncertainty examined. Record these levels distinctly.

Suggested literature-record fields: **domain; title; authors; year; DOI/URL; review or primary study; screening level; central claim; evidence; assumptions; candidate link; verification notes**.

Overlapping papers may contribute to multiple domains when their content substantively covers those domains; record the overlap.

## 2. Search for cross-disciplinary tensions

Search for conflicts that arise when findings are considered under a common interpretation. Candidate classes include:

| Evidence A | Evidence B | Example question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plate reconstructions and sea-level histories | Phylogenetic divergence dates | Did a proposed dispersal route exist during the relevant interval? |
| Fossil first/last appearances | Molecular-clock age estimates | Can the implied temporal ordering be reconciled? |
| Fossil geography | Ancestral-range reconstructions | Which dispersal or extinction assumptions explain the discrepancy? |
| Paleoclimate and habitat reconstructions | Adaptive or demographic claims | Does the proposed environment align with the inferred selective scenario? |
| Developmental constraints | Morphological transitions | Which proposed innovations require a different sequence of events? |
| Ancient genomes and admixture | Archaeological or fossil chronology | Are migration and divergence scenarios mutually compatible? |

Trace a tension to its underlying measurements and assumptions before treating it as a scientific contradiction.

Pay particular attention to **negative results, unresolved anomalies, alternative chronologies, unsuccessful model fits, contested classifications, and unexpected absences**.

## 3. Turn a connection into a question

For each promising candidate, write a short record:

1. **Question.** One sentence naming the event, lineage, region, or mechanism.
2. **Established premises.** Primary-source findings with dates, units, uncertainties, and definitions.
3. **Tension.** The specific combination of premises that puts pressure on an existing interpretation.
4. **Explanations.** Distinct candidate mechanisms, including measurement and model alternatives.
5. **Discriminating evidence.** An existing dataset, mathematical constraint, counterexample, or independent prediction.
6. **Next calculation.** The smallest useful analysis.

Prefer explicit temporal constraints and independent evidence wherever available. Express geological ages consistently in Ma before present, noting that larger Ma values indicate older events. Preserve original dating uncertainties and the provenance of reconstructions.

## 4. Final report

Produce **one report** using [REPORT_TEMPLATE.md](REPORT_TEMPLATE.md), with:

- The selected narrow research question and why it arises.
- A concise disciplinary literature map and source coverage.
- An evidence table connecting independently published results.
- The apparent conflict, its assumptions, and plausible resolutions.
- A candidate inference, derivation, or quantitative test.
- Reproducible inputs and methods for any calculations.
- A reference list with verified publication metadata and accessible source links.

A hypothesis is presented as a hypothesis; a demonstrated implication is accompanied by its derivation; empirical findings are accompanied by source data and a reproducible procedure.

The pilot's first deliverable is a **substantive synthesis report**. Research artifacts can be added as needed to support the selected question.
